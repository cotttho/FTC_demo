# Robot-Centric Driving Practice

## Two-Hour Full-Field Session

### Purpose

Build driver confidence with robot-centric mecanum controls, improve precise alignment, and develop repeatable full-field cycles under match pressure.

In robot-centric control, joystick directions are relative to the robot: pushing forward always moves the robot toward its current front, regardless of where it is facing on the field.

## Equipment and Setup

- Competition robot and charged batteries
- Driver Station and gamepads
- Full field with normal obstacles and scoring structures
- 8–12 cones, game pieces, or floor markers
- Tape for lanes and stopping boxes
- Stopwatch and score sheet

Before driving, inspect the robot, verify the drive controls and speed modes, and agree on a clear emergency-stop call. Nobody enters the field until the robot is disabled.

## 0:00–0:10 — Setup and Controls Check

1. Inspect the wheels, hubs, mechanisms, battery, and wire routing.
2. Clear the field and place markers at the corners, center, and important scoring or loading positions.
3. Confirm joystick mapping, drive direction, speed modes, and emergency stop.
4. Test forward, backward, left strafe, right strafe, and rotation.
5. Have each driver complete one slow lap around the field perimeter.

Do not continue until the robot responds predictably and the driver can stop it safely.

## 0:10–0:25 — Orientation Warm-Up

Place the robot at the center of the field.

1. Start with the robot facing the audience side. Drive to each wall using translation only.
2. Rotate the robot 90 degrees and repeat.
3. Repeat from 180-degree and 270-degree starting orientations.
4. Have the coach call a field location. The driver must choose the correct robot-centric input and move there.
5. Reset the robot to a different heading after every few calls.

**Success target:** 10 correct initial movements in a row, with no movement in the wrong direction.

## 0:25–0:45 — Precision Lanes

Use tape or cones to create two parallel lanes and a stopping box at each end.

1. Drive forward through a lane, stop in the box, and reverse back through it.
2. Strafe left and right through the second lane without rotating.
3. Drive a square: forward, strafe right, backward, then strafe left.
4. Repeat at slow, medium, and full speed.
5. Narrow the lanes after three clean runs.

Record the time, cones touched, and whether the robot finishes completely inside the stopping box. Prioritize a clean path before increasing speed.

## 0:45–1:05 — Slalom and Rotation

Place five or six cones across the field.

1. Slalom through the cones without rotating the robot.
2. Repeat while keeping the robot pointed toward one field wall.
3. Repeat with a 90-degree turn at every other cone.
4. Drive a figure eight around two widely spaced markers.
5. Run the course forward and then in reverse.

**Success target:** Three consecutive clean runs before increasing speed.

## 1:05–1:25 — Target Approach Drill

Mark several realistic scoring, pickup, or interaction positions.

For each target:

1. Start from the center or a realistic collection area.
2. Approach the target and stop in scoring alignment.
3. Complete the pickup or scoring action when the mechanism is available.
4. Back away cleanly without contacting the target.
5. Repeat from a different robot heading.

Practice forward, backward, sideways, and diagonal approaches. For a diagonal approach, require one controlled final correction before scoring.

**Success target:** 8 successful alignments out of 10 attempts.

## 1:25–1:45 — Full-Field Routes

Create a route with four to six stations, for example:

> Start → pickup area → scoring area → far corner → scoring area → park

1. Learn the route at 50% speed.
2. Repeat until the complete route is clean.
3. Increase speed without sacrificing control.
4. Alternate clockwise and counterclockwise routes.
5. Change the robot's starting orientation between runs.

Add time penalties to encourage controlled driving:

- Cone or field contact: **+2 seconds**
- Missed stopping or scoring position: **+3 seconds**
- Wrong initial direction or major correction: **+2 seconds**

## 1:45–1:57 — Timed Match Cycles

Run three rounds consisting of three minutes of driving and one minute of feedback or driver change.

Give the driver match-style objectives instead of a memorized path, such as:

> Collect from the near side, score twice, travel to the far side, then park.

During a run, the coach should use only the communication expected in a match. Record completed cycles, collisions, missed alignments, and moments of orientation confusion.

## 1:57–2:00 — Review

Each driver states:

- One skill that improved
- One recurring mistake
- One exercise to repeat during the next practice

Record the fastest clean route, average completed cycles, alignment success rate, and total collisions as the team's baseline.

## Coaching Priorities

1. **Accuracy before speed:** A direct controlled route is usually faster than a rushed route with several corrections.
2. **Look ahead:** Focus on the destination and field traffic instead of watching the robot continuously.
3. **Use deliberate headings:** Select a useful robot orientation before approaching a target.
4. **Make small final inputs:** Reduce speed near targets and avoid oscillating corrections.
5. **Reset after mistakes:** Stop, identify the robot's front, and deliberately choose the next input.
