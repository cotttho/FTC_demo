# Field-Centric Driving Practice

## Two-Hour Full-Field Session

### Purpose

Build driver confidence with field-centric mecanum controls, maintain consistent field-relative movement from any robot heading, and develop fast, repeatable match cycles.

In field-centric control, joystick directions stay aligned with the field: pushing the stick away from the driver moves the robot away from the driver even after the robot rotates. Rotation remains controlled separately.

## Equipment and Setup

- Competition robot and charged batteries
- Driver Station and gamepads
- Full field with normal obstacles and scoring structures
- 8–12 cones, game pieces, or floor markers
- Tape for lanes, stopping boxes, and a field-direction reference
- Stopwatch and score sheet

Mark the field's forward direction where the drivers can see it. Before driving, inspect the robot, confirm the configured forward direction, test heading reset, and agree on an emergency-stop call. Nobody enters the field until the robot is disabled.

## 0:00–0:10 — Setup, Heading, and Reset Check

1. Inspect the wheels, hubs, mechanisms, battery, and wire routing.
2. Clear the field and mark its forward, backward, left, and right directions.
3. Place the robot in the team's standard starting orientation.
4. Initialize the OpMode and verify that field-forward on the joystick produces field-forward motion.
5. Test forward, backward, left, and right from headings of 0, 90, 180, and 270 degrees.
6. Practice the heading-reset control twice using the team's exact reset procedure.
7. Have each driver complete one slow field-relative perimeter lap.

Do not continue if field directions are incorrect or inconsistent. Fix the initialization orientation or heading reference first.

## 0:10–0:25 — Field-Direction Warm-Up

Place the robot at the center of the field.

1. The coach calls “field forward,” “field backward,” “field left,” or “field right.”
2. The driver moves one robot length in that direction without rotating.
3. Rotate the robot 45–90 degrees and repeat the calls.
4. Move to each wall while the coach changes the robot's heading between attempts.
5. Finish with diagonal calls such as “forward-right” and “back-left.”

The driver should look at the field and destination rather than mentally converting directions from the robot's front.

**Success target:** 12 correct initial movements in a row from at least four different headings.

## 0:25–0:45 — Fixed-Heading Shapes

Use cones to mark a large square, diamond, and figure eight.

1. Drive the square while keeping the robot pointed toward the audience side.
2. Drive the same square while pointed toward the scoring side.
3. Drive the diamond without changing robot heading.
4. Drive the figure eight while holding a fixed heading.
5. Repeat each shape at slow and medium speed.

Watch for unintended rotation, curved paths, and abrupt corrections. If heading hold is automatic, the driver should learn how strongly it responds before increasing speed.

**Success target:** Three consecutive clean shapes with no cone contact and less than 10 degrees of unintended heading change.

## 0:45–1:05 — Move-and-Rotate Course

Place five or six cones across the field.

1. Strafe field-right while slowly rotating one full turn.
2. Drive field-forward while rotating 180 degrees, then stop in a marked box.
3. Slalom through the cones while continuously facing the center cone.
4. Drive a circle around a marker while keeping the robot pointed toward it.
5. Repeat the course in the opposite direction.

Begin with small rotation inputs. Translation should remain field-relative throughout the turn.

**Success target:** Three clean runs without a wrong-direction start or missed stopping box.

## 1:05–1:25 — Target-Facing Approaches

Mark several realistic scoring, pickup, or interaction positions.

For each target:

1. Start at the center or a realistic collection area with a random heading.
2. Drive toward the target using field-relative translation.
3. Rotate during travel so the mechanism reaches the required final heading.
4. Stop in scoring alignment and complete the action when possible.
5. Back away in a chosen field direction while maintaining awareness of traffic.

Practice facing the target, facing away for a rear mechanism, and holding a sideways orientation. Use one smooth arc when possible instead of translating and rotating as separate steps.

**Success target:** 8 successful alignments out of 10 attempts, with no more than one final correction per attempt.

## 1:25–1:43 — Full-Field Routes

Create a route with four to six stations, for example:

> Start → near pickup → far scoring area → center obstacle → near scoring area → park

1. Learn the route at 50% speed while using field-direction callouts.
2. Repeat until the complete route is clean.
3. Increase speed and rotate toward the next action while traveling.
4. Run mirrored routes from both alliance sides.
5. Change the robot's starting heading while keeping the route directions the same.

Add time penalties to reward controlled driving:

- Cone or field contact: **+2 seconds**
- Missed stopping or scoring position: **+3 seconds**
- Wrong field direction or major correction: **+2 seconds**
- Unnecessary heading reset: **+2 seconds**

## 1:43–1:55 — Heading Recovery Drill

This drill teaches recognition and safe recovery when the heading reference becomes inaccurate.

1. Place the robot safely in an open area and disable it.
2. Reposition it so the stored field heading is intentionally incorrect, or use the team's approved method to simulate an incorrect reference.
3. Enable the robot and have the driver identify the mismatch using one small test input.
4. Stop in a safe location, point the robot in the team's standard reset orientation, and perform the heading reset.
5. Confirm all four field directions before resuming normal driving.

Never surprise the driver with a heading-reference change while the robot is moving near people or field structures.

**Success target:** Recognize and safely correct the mismatch in under 15 seconds without collision.

## 1:55–2:00 — Timed Challenge and Review

Run one three-minute match-style challenge. Give objectives rather than a memorized path, and use only normal match communication. Record completed cycles, collisions, missed alignments, wrong-direction starts, and heading resets.

During the final two minutes, each driver states:

- One field-centric skill that improved
- One recurring mistake
- One exercise to repeat during the next practice

Record the results as the team's baseline.

## Coaching Priorities

1. **Trust field directions:** Drive toward the destination without converting through the robot's orientation.
2. **Separate the sticks mentally:** One stick commands field translation; the other commands robot rotation.
3. **Rotate with a purpose:** Select the heading needed for the next mechanism action or sight line.
4. **Use small test inputs after doubt:** Confirm the reference safely before accelerating.
5. **Reset deliberately:** Stop in open space, use the known orientation, reset, and verify.
6. **Accuracy before speed:** Increase speed only after paths and headings are repeatable.
